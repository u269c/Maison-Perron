Playtest fixes (1.2.1)

Your notes from playing 1.2, one by one, with the calls I made along the way.

DecisionBridges only where the road is cut

The road now dips into a real gully under each bridge: a dark far bank, water, earth faces and posts. The deck sits flush with the road ends, and grass tufts are no longer drawn on top of it. Alternative: keep the flat road and remove the bridges. The gully reads better and gives the stream a reason to be there.

DecisionA new game is a new game

Starting again now clears obols, nectar, letters, items, both hands and any pending report to Eurystheus. The restart prompt now says plainly that the whole adventure will be lost.

DecisionSwap the weapon rows, not the buttons

Throwing weapons are now the left row and melee the right, so L1 cycles the left group and R1 the right. Button bindings didn't change, so nobody has to relearn them.

DecisionScenes wait for each other, and for you to be alive

Brigands now hold off until their introduction has played. A story scene that comes up while the hero is dead waits until he respawns instead of running over the death screen. The Delphi spring dialog no longer draws dark text over the black fade.

DecisionThe knock and the staff swing

The boy walks up to the jar and his knuckles reach it. Mentor does a real overhead staff swing: wind up, strike level, recoil, hold, lower. The jar reacts on impact, not before.

DecisionSnow, gradually, and why

Snowy patches start appearing on the ground and in the firs over about 300 px, and the snowfall ramps up smoothly. When it starts, the hero and Mentor remark on it: Erymanthos is the mountain where the snow never melts, and where Heracles drove his boar into deep snow. That's also the hint for the fight.

DecisionBig snowdrifts, and a boar with his head in them

The drifts are now real piles, almost the hero's height, lumpy and shaded, with a twig sticking out. When the boar charges in, the pile wobbles, snow flies, and his front half disappears into it, leaving his rump, wiggling body and frantic little tail sticking out until he backs out. Each plunge flattens the pile a little, and they never get too small to use. Alternative: a stun star over his head. The buried rump is funnier and shows clearly that now is the moment to hit.

DecisionAn honest boss bar

You weren't imagining it: the boar's bar counted his whole hit budget, but he only needs two good hits while stuck (three on Heroic). The bar now uses that number from the first hit, and fights that count partial progress (hits, ties) show it in the bar.

DecisionArtemis can be talked to

She watches the hunt and is only visible in God's view, so she can now be talked to in God's view. She has three lines, depending on where you are in the labour: before the hind, before the boar, and after.

DecisionEurystheus, clearer

Now: “Nemea is to the east. Bring me the lion’s skin. Not too close, mind. Leave it by the jar and knock twice.” Same joke (he's scared), and the sentence makes sense.

CheckRuns after the fixes

8 of 8 full bot playthroughs finished (kid profile, with and without monkeys, with quit-and-Continue). Four more runs crashed the browser because I ran too many at once on this machine. They passed when rerun two at a time.

Hardening (1.2)

You asked for a few hundred full-game runs with screenshots, monkeys included, to harden the game. Here's what ran, what broke and what I changed. I didn't change any story or balance decisions except where noted.

MethodHow the runs work

  • Full game every time. A bot starts a new game, then plays the Prologue, Delphi, the labours, the sea, Crete, Act III and Act IV through to the end card. A director tells it where the story goes when "walk right" isn't it (the goat tree, Pan and Mentor in the God's view, and so on).
  • Players: "kid" (slow, often facing the wrong way, no reading of tells), "hardcore" (reads every tell), "masher" and Heroic.
  • Monkeys on top: random keys, holding a direction, God's-view spam, the pause menu (Heroic, language, music, the Library), the Book, skipping scenes by holding Esc, mashing talk. Light is about 0.6 events a second, heavy about 2.2.
  • Quit and Continue: a real page reload, then Continue from the title, at random moments, or in every single level for the "Continue everywhere" runs.
  • Checks every frame: a scene that never ends, a dialog that never closes, dead without a respawn, a black screen with nothing playing, NaN positions, falling through the floor, the hero off-screen, followers standing inside the hero, floods of objects. A run that makes no story progress for 4 minutes counts as stuck. If the hero or the boss is still trading hits, it counts as a grind (too hard for that bot) rather than a softlock.
  • Screenshots at random levels and at every failure. A subagent reviewed a sample of 40.
  • The simulator runs about 40 times faster than real time (one full game in about a minute), so 385 runs fit in an afternoon.

FixesSoftlocks and state bugs it found

  • Quit after the last labour, before the king's report: on Continue there was no way to Act II ("Nothing more that way"). The pending report is now saved and replays in Tiryns.
  • Quit on Olympus after the fight: Hera came back as an enemy you couldn't fight, and the ending never came. Now Olympus resumes the homecoming, or the epilogue if you'd already chosen.
  • Scenes started on a real-time timer (setTimeout) in about 15 places: Hephaestus arriving after the tripods, the winds' bag scene, the serpents' aftermath, the eye from Athena, boss respawns, Typhon's chain, the nectar drink. Pausing at the wrong moment could make them fire behind the menu or get lost. They now run on game time, stop while paused, and are dropped if you leave the level.
  • Story scenes could be silently dropped if another scene or a chat was running at that instant. That could mean a boss beaten with no ending scene, or an exit that never fires. Story scenes now wait their turn or interrupt a chat with a villager. Zones no longer get used up by a cutscene walking through them.
  • Fades, cards, constellations and the respawn were timed by drawing frames, not game time. On a slow or busy machine a constellation could hang, and so could the hero after a death. They're timed by the game now.
  • Every fade-in after a fade-out was invisible: the screen stayed black for the whole fade, then popped. Fixed everywhere. Transitions now actually fade in.
  • A scene ending early could leave the hero walking on his own. He's released now.
  • The Owl's Eye could come back on a level change between the eye breaking on the shore and "It was only bronze".

FixesFrom the screenshot review

  • The Hydra hint covered the boss name, so hints now sit above the boss bar during fights.
  • Mentor and the companion often started a level piled onto the hero. They now line up behind him.
  • "Got: a torch" style pop-ups no longer float over a black screen. English and Greek use "Music: off", French keeps "Musique : non".
  • "Restart the Prologue" really restarted the whole adventure. It now says "Start again from the beginning", with a matching warning.

DecisionMercy for the two fights that could go on forever

The fuzzing showed that a flailing child could get stuck for a very long time at the Hydra (heads double faster than you burn them) and at Circe (no damage, so no respawn mercy either). In normal mode only:
  • Hydra: once she has three heads, a cut regrows one instead of two. After 45 s the regrowth slows down. If you die, the extra heads wither back to the starting number.
  • Circe: each missed cauldron window makes her stir half a second longer (up to 4 times), and after two misses a hint explains it.
Heroic is unchanged.

DecisionThe Hydra, after the Caeretan hydria

From your reference: one snake, no big body. The trunk loops into a coil, and a fan of thin necks grows from one root. The heads are plain, gaping snake heads with fangs and a forked tongue when they strike. In the God's view the necks alternate black and added red, with incised lines, like the vase. The immortal head wears a small crown (added white, or gold in the mortal view). Head positions are unchanged, so the fight plays the same. The Book of the Gods painting uses the same drawing.
To match the vase even more closely, the heads could fan straight up. They'd then be out of reach until they bite, which changes the fight, so I asked first.

FixesEvery run that stopped in the final batches, and what happened

QuestionHeroic Aeolus, Circe and Talos

The hardcore bot finished 5 of 10 full games on Heroic. Every stop was a grind at the winds, Circe or Talos. The Heroic winds (blowing 190 px/s, only 1.2 s tired) need the trick of slipping behind a wind while it inhales, which the bot doesn't know, and maybe most players won't either. I left Heroic alone. Should Heroic winds get a slightly longer tired window, or a hint about getting behind them?

Act IV (1.1)

DecisionThe pool leads straight on to Etna

After ΗΦΑΙΣΤΟΥ settles, the screen fades and you're at Etna. No return to the deck: the boat story ended with Act III. Hephaestus is at a forge at the foot of the volcano. The reunion is short and awkward on purpose: "I make things. I never learned how to make a father." Then the mountain wakes up.

DecisionTyphon is a team fight with your father

Typhon fills the right side of the arena. He breathes fire low (jump) or high (stay down), drops lava, and slams a fist where a ring warns. After the slam, Hephaestus throws a chain over the fist ("Sit DOWN, brother!") and you hit the snake head on the wrist, marked by a pulsing ring. It's the only fight where the boss is beaten by both of you. Hephaestus then forges the bonds he'll need for Hera, and Dionysus turns up on his donkey.
The alternative was Hephaestus watching from the side. A fight where his chain opens the window felt like the right first thing for them to do together.

DecisionArgus at the Heraion: the eyes that were watching you

Hera's peacock carries Argus's hundred eyes, and Mentor tells you it's been watching all along. It sees both worlds, so switching views doesn't hide you. Its fanned tail drops columns of light one after another, and you stand between them. Then it's dazzled by its own show (dizzy stars) and stumbles toward you, and you strike. When beaten it closes all its eyes, one by one, and falls asleep. "A hundred years on guard. Let him sleep."

DecisionHera: one fight, four kinds of sight

  • The guest rule. You drew water for her at the well in Act I, so she won't strike first. You have to hit her once to start the fight ("You strike first. Good. My house debts are paid.").
  • The Queen: eyes thrown in arcs, then a charge. You jump over her, her sceptre sticks in the marble with dizzy stars, and you hit.
  • Madness 1: four Heras. Your mortal eyes lie, and only the God's view shows the real one.
  • Madness 2: the reverse. Now the names lie (all four say ΗΡΑ), and only the mortal view shows her shadow.
  • The crack: the vase splits. You find your father's three bonds in the God's view and drive them in with mortal eyes. "These are HIS bonds. The same ones."
This uses everything the game taught about the two views, then asks for both at once. No new controls.

DecisionThe ending: she has to look

Held, Hera says she's never seen him, because mortals are mist to her. So he makes her look: the boy from the well, his father's hands, and the name beside him. Ocyrhoe hears her sentence finish ("…of the blood of Hera") and her curse starts to lift. Your last choice follows your stance: Force makes her swear on the Styx ("It hears everything, the gossip"). Cunning trades three bonds for three favours: Ocyrhoe keeps her shape, the stars stay up, nobody hides any more. Heart frees her for nothing, and gives her back an apple from her orchard: "On Olympus we take. We don't give back."

DecisionThe homecoming, the loophole, the name

Dionysus delivers Hephaestus ("One smith, slightly grumpy, mildly tipsy. Already paid. By me. In wine."). Hera can't claim him because no god may claim a mortal child. Mentor, very tall and very polite in the God's view, points out that the oath says nothing about grandchildren. Hera shouts it to the whole sky. The real Heracles shows up to apologise about the lion ("I did kill it. Properly."). Hera admits she brought it back. Then she names the boy: Heracles got "glory of Hera" and resented it, so he gets his own. "Pericles… no. Perroclès. The glory of Perrhaebia." Hebe offers nectar: drink and stay at your father's forge on Olympus, or go home to your mother.

DecisionTwo epilogues

Olympus: "On Sundays, a woman from Perrhaebia visits her son at the forge. Hera pours her nectar herself. She's trying." Home: Oloosson at dusk with his mother, and "sometimes a limping tinker comes by, and the pots cost nothing." Both end with the full sky, the God's view, Τέλος, and the end card.

DecisionHephaestus now carries a smith's hammer

He had a club in the first draft, which made him read as Heracles. The image reviewers caught it.

Act III (1.0)

DecisionHera's reveal opens Act III, on the shore

The adopted story puts it at the end of Act II, on Circe's shore. I'd skipped it, so it now opens Act III. The captain drops you on the grey shore of Ocean, and the old woman is waiting: "Have you found him, the dangerous boy with golden eyes?" The hero switches to the God's view, reads ΗΡΑ clearly for the first time, and she sees his gold eyes and the owl. "Oh. …It's you. The kind boy. That makes it so much worse." Mentor steps in ("Hera. Leave him.") and she answers "Athena. Of course. You always did like strays." She can't touch him under the oath, so she crushes the amulet instead, after "I'm sorry, dear". Later, by the fire: "It was only bronze." He lifts the mist with his empty hand and gets the God's view back for good, with a slightly bigger meter.

DecisionLemnos: catch the furniture, then the forge-blade

Three self-moving tripods on golden wheels (they're in the Iliad) run away from you. Hit them or throw a stone to stop them. Hephaestus, without turning round: "They run off when I have guests. …I never have guests." He insults the xiphos's edge ("Who sharpened it, a goat?") and reforges it into the forge-blade: +1 damage, a slash with a cold blue trail. "I'm not good with words. I'm good with hinges." Asked why he pays for everything, he starts "Because you… Because I…" and thunder answers: "The Styx isn't stupid." Mentor tells the hero to look beside him with his own eyes, and the tablet reads ΗΦΑΙΣΤΟΣ, matching his ΗΦΑ. Hephaestus: "Lots of names start with Η."

DecisionThe far West: Atlas's sky gag, then Ladon

Atlas, counting returned stars, offers the classic deal: hold the sky "one little minute" while he fetches an apple. The hero holds a starry dome for about 20 s and keeps it level with ← →. If it tips, "Whoa! Sorry, sky!", and you lose 2 s. Atlas comes back with no apple ("The dragon bit me. You're so good at this, I think I'll just retire"). You choose the cunning way out ("Could you take it back a second? I need to fix my cushion") or the heart way ("Please. I have someone to find."). Then Ladon, Hera's star-dragon, coiled round her tree. His head draws back low (jump) or high (stay on the ground). You can also step out of his neck's reach. After a lunge he pants in the grass, and you hit him. Every fifth move is a tail sweep you jump over. He rises as Draco and leaves the letter Ι. A Hesperid gives Hera's side of the story: the orchard was "the only thing anyone ever gave her that was just hers". You take one golden apple ("She counts them").
The first Ladon was far too hard: the lunge reached everywhere, and simulated kids died up to 13 times. It now has shorter reach, longer openings, a tail sweep on one side only, and he tires after each of your deaths. Kids: 10–28 s, no deaths.

DecisionPrometheus and the Eagle; Hermes brings the obol

Prometheus is free (Heracles came by, "not a word, and I'd made biscuits"). He wears a ring made from the last link of his chain, by "the god who was sorry", the smith who wept at every hammer blow when he chained him. That matches Aeschylus, where Hephaestus chains Prometheus unwillingly. The Eagle still circles, hungry. Its shadow marks the dive, its talons stick in the snow so you can hit it, and it drops feathers from above. It rises as Aquila with Σ. Hermes then delivers Charon's obol, "already paid. Obviously", with a message from the customer: "Eat before you go down."

DecisionThe Underworld

  • The two views swap roles here. Below, the God's view doesn't drain the meter, and the dead show only that way, painted white as on funeral lekythoi. The mortal view is just gloom.
  • Charon: "Obol. The living pay double, but you look half divine, so… one. Ferryman's maths."
  • Cerberus is not a fight. Each head cycles calm → growl → snap. Pet a calm head twice to put it to sleep. All three must sleep at once, then he rolls over for a belly rub: "All three want a belly rub. He only has one belly." It pays off Ocyrhoe's prophecy.
  • Heracles' phantom gives advice one act too late ("The lion: no sword. …Done? The Hydra, then…"), then a real one: "When you know the name, don't shout it."
  • Tiresias: "I'm blind, not late." He's glad the man with the oar finally listened, and sends you to the clear pool, not Lethe.
  • The pool of Memory: a vision of a limping man leaving a tiny bronze owl in a cradle without coming in. Then Τ Ο Υ, and the letters line up as ΗΦΑΙΣΤΟΥ. A golden hammer mark glows over his heart in the God's view. End of build 1.0.
Letters: Ι from Ladon, Σ from the Eagle, then Τ Ο Υ at the pool. The story doc's scrambled-letters version, with the "Who could it be?" suspect scroll and Dionysus as second suspect, is still available if you want the mystery harder. I kept the in-order reveal we already had.

DecisionHera breaks the Owl's Eye, but the item row stays honest

After the crush, the Eye's meter disappears until "It was only bronze". After that, the same switch and meter come back as his own sight. I didn't add a new icon.

Act II (0.7)

DecisionHow Act II starts

When Eurystheus runs out of labours (after the stables), Mentor says the beasts of the land are back in the sky but Hera "always has the sea", and the hero answers "Then we take a ship." Argos harbour follows: Hera is again the old woman, and she almost recognises Mentor ("All old people look alike"). The captain says the trip is already paid for by "a fellow who limped, smelled of the forge, with hands like anvils", the third clue about the father.

DecisionOcyrhoe is a stowaway behind the crates, and a quiet companion

Chiron's daughter, cursed for telling the future: she's turning into a mare "a bit more every year; so far only the bottom half". In play she follows a little behind, shoves foes that get close, and heals 1 heart when you're at 2 or less (every 22 s; every 40 s in Heroic). She also makes approximate prophecies ("You're going to trip. …Now. …No. Later."), and an occasional neigh slips out. She keeps her distance from Mentor and stands on the far side from bosses so she doesn't hide the hero.
Alternative: a companion you control with a button. I avoided it because the pad has no free face button.

DecisionIsland order follows the stance, like the labours

Force: Cyclopes → Aeolus → Circe. Cunning: Aeolus → Circe → Cyclopes. Heart: Circe → Cyclopes → Aeolus. The ship's deck is the hub between islands, and the captain names the next stop.

DecisionPolyphemus: "Nobody" is a choice, and the giant names you anyway

He asks your name, and you choose between "I don't have a name" (heart), "Nobody" (cunning) and "Your worst nightmare" (force). Whichever you pick, he ends up calling you Nobody, which fits the nameless hero. Maron's wine is optional: it's in a wreck on the beach. If you have it, Mentor pours it and the giant gets tipsy and slower (3 punishes instead of 4, longer openings).

DecisionThe cyclops fight

A dust ring shows where his club will land. It sticks in the ground for 2.4 s, and two hits on it "jar his fingers" (one punish). Four punishes (3 with the wine, 5 in Heroic) and he sits down for a nap. Standing at his feet doesn't hurt you: he flicks you away ("Shoo! Not on my toes!") and then slams where you landed. Kids in the sims kept walking up to his legs, and the first version killed them about 20 times per fight. If you die, he gets a little more tired each time (longer openings, up to twice). The olive stake, "NOBODY has put out my eye!", the neighbours shouting back "If nobody hurt you, stop shouting", and the escape under the ram ("Why are you the last one out today?") all play as a scene, with no gameplay under the sheep.
Alternative: a stealth sequence under the sheep. It could be added later as a short bonus.

DecisionAeolus: four runaway winds instead of a boss

The winds have escaped from his bag: four puffy clouds with cheeks (Boreas, Notos, Euros, Zephyros). Each one inhales, then blows you backwards unless you shelter behind a bronze windbreak. Out of breath, it sinks low enough to hit, and only then. Two hits and it goes back in the bag ("Not the bag! Not the bag!"). One wind blows at a time, and the nearest one follows you so none get lost behind you. Aeolus then gives the bag with one rule, "DO NOT OPEN IT. Everyone opens it." On the deck, a sailor opens it, thinking it's cheese. "WHO OPENED THE BAG?!" "Nobody!" "That one's already taken." There's no penalty: the wind blows them where they were going anyway.
The bag isn't an inventory item, so the 10-icon limit is untouched (8 items planned).

DecisionCirce: knock over the cauldron, don't hit the lady

Hermes delivers the moly ("already paid", "tastes like an old sock; that's how you know it works"). Circe throws two potion flasks, then goes back to stir her cauldron. Hit the cauldron twice while she stirs to tip it over. After three spills (4 in Heroic) she runs out of ingredients and gives up. A flask or her wand turns you into a piglet for a moment (1.6 s with moly, 4 s without): you can still run, but you can't attack. Only in Heroic does her wand cost a heart. Her wolves are tame and want belly rubs, and the pigs are your sailors ("Oink. I mean… help."). Last line: she knows your father, "a man who works with his hands. He made me that cauldron. You just dented it."
Why non-violent: hitting Circe herself felt wrong for the tone, and the cauldron gives a clear, readable target.

DecisionFixes found while testing

  • A scene could freeze forever if its speech bubble was cleared (that caused the 3 kid timeouts in the Act I pass). Fixed.
  • Gates such as "The hind first" only blocked you once. They now block every time.
  • Travelling to a new place from inside a scene failed silently and left the game stuck. It now waits for the scene to end.
  • About 24 sound effects (roar, clang, splash) were silently falling back to a footstep. They now have real sounds.
  • When the hind and Mentor were both in reach, Talk always picked Mentor. The hind now wins.
  • Crouching to pick something up: the back knee went through the floor and the hand never reached the ground. Both are fixed, with a deeper bow and a visible bend in both knees.
  • Mentor's staff ran through his face. It's now held a little further forward.
  • Act III: Ladon and the Eagle used to attack before their fight started (Ladon lunged at you while you were holding the sky). Fixed.
  • Bosses that don't clear their own hit flash (the cyclops, Talos, Circe) blinked half-transparent for the rest of the fight after the first hit. A reviewer saw a "ghostly" Polyphemus and Talos. Fixed.

DecisionThe passages are played on the deck

After the three isles, the captain sails on to the Sirens and then the strait. Both take place on the ship, with the scenery passing behind it. That keeps them short and readable and reuses the hub.

DecisionThe Sirens: wax, one ball at a time

Their song pulls you and three sailors slowly toward the rail. Holding ← resists, but only halfway. The beeswax barrel is at the stern. You carry one ball at a time and talk to a sailor to plug his ears ("WHY AM I SHOUTING?"). If a sailor or you reaches the rail, the captain hauls them back: "Not on my ship!" / "You too?! You're twelve!". There's no damage and no failure, only time lost. Mentor asks to be tied to the mast "because I've always wanted to hear them". Afterwards he describes the song as "a lyre falling down the stairs. But a very beautiful lyre." Ocyrhoe hums off-key on purpose.
Alternative: a rhythm mini-game against the song. Maybe later, as a bonus.

DecisionScylla and Charybdis: a progress bar, not a boss

A bar at the top fills over about 50 s as the ship gets through. A shadow on the deck marks where a head will strike. Step aside, and the head stays stuck in the planks for about 1 s. Two hits send it back to its cave for good and push the bar forward. Every 11–15 s Charybdis tilts the ship and you slide toward the whirlpool side unless you hold →. A strike costs 1 heart, the only damage in this part of Act II. Nobody is lost, unlike Odysseus' six men, and Mentor notes the gods will start talking about you, "not necessarily good news". The captain counts one sailor too many: it's a seagull.

DecisionTalos: the nail at the ankle, and the third letter

Talos asks for papers: "No papers. And you, no name. That's two problems. I solve problems with rocks." Mentor recalls that Medea talked him out of his nail, then adds "we don't have the time". He fights like a bronze version of the cyclops. His slam marks a spot. After it he stays bent over and the nail at his ankle glows: hit it twice to loosen it, three times over (4 in Heroic). Up close he stomps, sending shockwaves you jump over. He throws rocks at range. Dying, he recognises the hammer mark on your blade, the same smith as his nail: "Tell the one who made me the nail itched. He always scratches at the things he loves." The nail carries the letter Α, so the father's name now reads ΗΦΑ.
Kids: 28–60 s, a death in 2 of 6 runs. Heroic hardcore 67–161 s with no deaths, because the bot is cautious. That's the second Heroic boss that may need more bite (see the Polyphemus question).

DecisionAriadne and Asterion follow Borges

Ariadne calls the Minotaur by his name, Asterion ("star"), her half-brother. She's bitter about Theseus ("He even forgot to change his sails. Heroes, honestly."). Asterion "came back, because stars don't die, they get lost". The Labyrinth is three rooms of three doors. ↑ goes through a door. A wrong door is "a cupboard" or "an echo that said 'lost'", and the thread crosses it out so you never retry it. The graffiti includes "THESEUS WAS HERE", "Daedalus: I regret nothing" and "14 doors. Or infinitely many. — A.". Asterion is a gentle, lonely counter of doors, not a fight.

DecisionOne companion ashore, chosen in the Labyrinth

The story plan says one companion is active at a time. Asterion asks to come, and you choose: Asterion comes ashore (heart) or Ocyrhoe stays (cunning). The other rides on the ship, with a line of their own ("I'm up to four thousand waves. Or three."). Asterion doesn't heal. He shoves foes and talks about counting the sky.
Alternative: swap companions freely on the deck. That's easy to add later if you prefer.

DecisionHow Act II ends

On the deck, the captain lists everything they survived, "and the whole crew is still here". The hero has three letters, Η Φ Α, and asks who can tell him the rest. Mentor: "The living have sworn to keep quiet. The dead have sworn nothing at all." Ocyrhoe sees "a dog with three heads. All three want to be petted." The captain agrees to drop them at the edge of the Underworld "and not one step further". End of build 0.9, pointing to Act III.

DecisionThe lion's pelt stays at +2 hearts

Simulated kids die 0.12 times per Hydra run with the pelt and 0.75 without, and both groups finish with 1–4 hearts left. It helps without trivialising the next labour.
Alternative: +1 heart, plus a damage reduction while wearing it. Easy to switch if it feels too generous in play.

DecisionThe Hydra: heads within reach, an opening after each bite

The first version was nearly unwinnable for kids (about 45 deaths per run): resting heads hovered out of reach, so you could only hit a head while it was biting you. Now resting heads sway at head height. A head that bites stays stuck in the mud for 0.7 s, and that's your opening. One head attacks at a time (two in Heroic). You start against 3 mortal heads plus the immortal one, which regrow in 6.5 s if not burned, up to 6 heads.
Result: kids 16/16, 0.44 deaths, median fight 22 s. Hardcore 0.25 deaths, 21 s. Heroic 1.6 deaths, 39 s.

DecisionTorch use is contextual

Swinging near a fresh stump burns it shut, with no extra button. The torch shows over the hero's shoulder while carried. It isn't one of the 7 weapon slots.
Alternative: a dedicated tool button. I avoided it because the pad has no free face button left.

DecisionThe arc-sling is a Hermes "delivery, already paid", and the venom is for stones

It keeps the running gag about the mysterious payer (your father) and matches Proposal B: the arc-sling is the Hydra reward and works only in the God's view. Instead of arrows, the hero dips his sling stones (with a stick) for double damage.

DecisionThe labour order follows the stance

Force: Nemea → Lerna → Arcadia. Cunning: Lerna → Arcadia → Nemea. Heart: Arcadia → Nemea → Lerna. The Augean stables always come last. Eurystheus' first assignment line changes with your answer. After each labour you return to Tiryns, knock, and he reacts (faints at the pelt, complains about the marsh smell, panics at a sacred hind, then argues that the stables only half count).

DecisionCatching the hind: creep with ↓

Hold ↓ while walking to creep (on a stick, push it gently). She bolts if you run or swing near her, and looks up if you move while she watches: stop and she goes back to grazing. Safety net: after four bolts she's out of breath and stops fleeing. Catch her by pressing Talk next to her.
Alternative: a "sneak" button. I chose ↓ because it was free and reads as crouching.

DecisionArtemis' favour: stones fly true

After the hind, sling stones home onto the nearest foe ahead, even a flying one. That makes the Stymphalian birds a sling fight, as in the myth. It's the "favour" icon in the item row.

DecisionThe boar: snowdrifts and Pholus

The boar charges. If his charge ends in a snowdrift he's stuck for about 4 s; hit him 3 times. Do that twice (3 times in Heroic) and he's worn out. Drifts glint while he paws the ground. After three misses, Pholus shouts and the drifts grow (the "safety net and snowdrift cue" from the open items). Pholus offers wine, Mentor replies "He's twelve," and Pholus offers snow water instead. He also minds the hind while you fight.

DecisionThe krotala rattles come from Athena, made by "a smith with very big hands"

That's faithful to Apollodorus (Athena gave Heracles bronze rattles made by Hephaestus) and another sly father clue. The hero does Athena's charade dance to shake them.

DecisionThe stables: two dams, a sluice, a flood, and Augeas doesn't pay

Faithful to the myth. Eurystheus first refuses to count it ("you asked to be paid"), then grudgingly says it counts "half, like the hydra". As the yard dries, the first two letters of the father's name appear painted on the clean wall: Η Φ. Only the God's view shows them, and Mentor "has nothing against you reading them".

DecisionThe lion's den: option 1 (rockfall), as you chose

The two-mouthed-cave "cunning" route is kept for a later Nemea revisit.

DecisionBeasts: legs, knees and ground contact

Beast legs were locking straight and floating 3–4 px (the lion) because the bones were too short for the body. Each beast now has its own bone lengths. Front legs stand straight with any slack at the elbow and fold at the wrist only when a paw lifts; hind legs bend at the hock. New beasts: the hind (golden antlers), the Erymanthian boar (tusks, bristle crest) and cows (plus Pholus, Artemis and Augeas).
