Eight real worlds, one star: the game takes place around Kepler-90

Kepler-90 is the only star besides the Sun known to have eight planets. Small rocky worlds sit close in and giants farther out, like a mini Solar System squeezed inside Earth's orbit. The game hops across all eight.

The route: inward toward the star, then the long way home

The campaign starts on a moon of the outermost planet, where temperatures are Earth-like, and dives inward. Every hop is hotter than the last, so the difficulty curve follows real physics. Hard mode flies back out.

Hard mode runs the same stops in reverse, from Brasier back to Havre, through darker versions of each world.

The music of the orbits

The five outer planets go round in near-whole-number ratios: their periods compare like 2 : 3 : 4 : 7 : 11. That is a real polyrhythm, ready-made for the soundtrack.

Press play to hear it: each planet ticks once per orbit, sped up so that one day lasts 4 milliseconds. Use it as the base of the punk tracks, a click the drummer locks to, or the planet-hop sting.

The Empire: Kepler-80, a court that moves like clockwork

The Emperor D'Justeece rules from Kepler-80, a cool orange-red dwarf about 1,223 light-years away in Cygnus. Its six planets are locked in one of the tightest resonant chains known.

Five of them (d, e, b, c, g) repeat the same dance every ~191 days, their periods in a ratio that reduces to 4 : 6 : 9 : 12 : 18. An empire of order and justice, whose planets literally keep perfect time. Kepler-80g was found by the same Google neural network, in the same study, as Kepler-90i: the two systems were announced together in December 2017.

Distance between the two systems: roughly 1,600 light-years. That's our own estimate from their sky positions and distances; both sit in the patch of sky the Kepler telescope stared at.

What's science, what's ours

  • No photos exist. These planets were found by the dips they cause in their star's light. Every picture of them, here and at NASA, is an illustration.
  • No proper names. The planets are only b to i. Havre, Guimauve and the rest are our game names.
  • Masses of b to f are estimates from their size, not measurements. Only g and h have measured masses (2025).
  • The space stations and life forms are invented, shaped by each world's conditions.
  • Havre, the home moon, is invented. No moons have been detected around Kepler-90 h.
  • Temperatures are "equilibrium" values, the heat from the star alone, with no greenhouse effect. Real surfaces could be hotter.
  • Tidal locking (one face always toward the star) is likely for the three inner rocky planets at those distances, but not measured.
  • What the sub-Neptunes are made of (thick gas, steam, deep oceans) is unknown: the level themes pick one possibility each.
